# What makes a personal AI assistant genuinely useful on a phone, and what has failed (2023–2026)

Scope note: researched 2026-10-10. Most hard numbers in this space come from vendor claims, one-off marketplace surveys or press reviews; peer-reviewed longitudinal data on *phone* assistant use in the LLM era is thin. Each finding is labelled where it is a vendor claim, a secondary report, or historical (pre-2023).

## 1. Which assistant use cases do people actually use daily on phones, and which do they abandon?

### Takeaway
Classic voice assistants are still used for the same short, hands-busy commands they were in 2018 (weather, music, quick answers, timers, calls, texting, directions, especially in the car); breadth of use shrinks over time and most "smart" features go undiscovered or are judged low-value. LLM chat apps are used for something different (advice, information, writing), and voice in those apps produces longer conversations, but that is open-ended talk, not task execution.

### Cited Findings
- YouGov (US, reported Aug 2025): top voice-assistant uses are weather 59%, music 51%, quick answers/web search 47%, timers/alarms 40%, hands-free calls 39%; usage patterns described as "virtually unchanged" since 2018 despite upgrades to Siri, Alexa and Google Assistant — [Implicator.ai summary of YouGov](https://www.implicator.ai/weather-music-timers-americans-still-use-assistants-like-its-2018.md)
- Same YouGov survey: 27% say their assistant doesn't understand what they say; 12% say the answers are inaccurate; "most people who own voice assistants aren't doing much with them" — [Royal Examiner](https://royalexaminer.com/?p=142977) (secondary; original YouGov page not opened)
- Historical (2020): music, general questions, weather, timers/alarms remained smart-speaker "killer apps" and third-party voice app (skill) usage was not growing — [Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2020/05/03/streaming-music-questions-weather-timers-and-alarms-remain-smart-speaker-killer-apps-third-party-voice-app-usage-not-growing/)
- Historical (2016, Creative Strategies): 98% of US iPhone users had tried Siri, but only 3% had used it in public (embarrassment); car use highest at 62%, home 39%, work 1.3% — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2016/06/06/siri-embarrassment-poll/)
- Historical (2017, Verto Analytics): Siri lost 7.3M US monthly users (~15%) in a year, and remaining users used it less often — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2017/07/11/apple-siri-usage)
- Commonly repeated stats (secondary, original surveys not verified): 62% of US smartphone users use voice assistants while driving; smartphone (56%) is the most used voice-assistant device vs smart speaker (35%) and TV (34%) — [DemandSage compilation](https://www.demandsage.com/voice-search-statistics/); treat as indicative only
- SellCell survey (Dec 2024, >2,000 US adults, ~1,000 each with AI-capable iPhones/Galaxies): 73% of iPhone and 87% of Galaxy users said AI features add "little to no value"; 58.4% of iPhone users hadn't explored Apple Intelligence (36.7% cited perceived lack of utility; 18.2% tried and found it inaccurate). Most-valued features: iPhone Writing Tools 72%, Notification summaries 54%; Galaxy Circle to Search 82.1%, Photo Assist 55.5%. Note: sources disagree on whether the 73/87% base is all owners or only AI users — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/12/16/most-iphone-owners-see-little-to-no-value-in-apple-intelligence-so-far/), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/new-survey-suggests-the-vast-majority-of-iphone-and-samsung-galaxy-users-find-ai-useless-and-to-be-honest-im-not-surprised), [Gigazine](https://wbgsv0b.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250330-iphone-galaxy-ai-survey)
- OpenAI/NBER "How People Use ChatGPT" (WP 34255, Sept 2025; authors include OpenAI staff): ~10% of world adults used ChatGPT by July 2025; non-work share of messages grew from 53% to >70%; Practical Guidance, Seeking Information and Writing together are nearly 80% of conversations; value lies mainly in decision support — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255), [OpenAI blog](https://openai.com/blog/how-people-are-using-chatgpt)
- Vendor claim (Google, I/O 2025): Gemini Live (voice) conversations are ~5x longer than text chats (2.5x claimed in Sept 2024) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/snippet/3009762/people-are-chatting-with-gemini-live-a-lot), [Droid-Life 2024](https://droid-life.com/2024/09/12/gemini-live-goes-free-to-use-for-android-users/amp)
- Abandonment: CHI 2020 "Alexa is a Toy" (38 older adults, 8 focus groups): most became non-users over time due to difficulty finding valuable uses, beliefs about ability, and shared-space problems — [IU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/10aaf444-3a20-4708-9e07-08064c84b137)
- IMWUT 2020 (Echo Dots in 10 families for a month): breadth of use decreases steadily over time; families learn functions by trial and error, asking the device about itself, and from friends/family — reported only in a search-result summary; the paper itself was not opened and its URL was not captured, so verify before citing (closest related source: [arXiv 2306.05741](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05741))
- Regression risk when assistants are "upgraded": Gemini replacing Google Assistant broke routines, timers and smart-home control for some users; Android Authority found Gemini for Home executed multi-part commands correctly about half the time; 22.1% of 136 Gemini-blaming Google Home reviews said a routine/automation that used to run no longer did (small self-selected blog sample) — [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/2857872/google-assistants-been-having-a-rough-few-weeks-heres-googles-response.html), [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-for-google-home-impressions-3633043), [Cybershack](https://cybershack.com.au/other/gemini-replaces-assistant/), [unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/google-home-gemini-voice-commands-worse-reviews-2026)
- Historical (2022): Business Insider reported Amazon's devices unit (mostly Alexa) on pace to lose ~$10B/year; most users stuck to weather, lights and music; a former employee called Alexa "a colossal failure of imagination" — [Popular Science](https://popsci.com/technology/amazon-alexa-lose-money), [Slashdot/Ars](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/22/11/21/2215257/amazon-alexa-is-a-colossal-failure-on-pace-to-lose-10-billion-this-year)

### Inferences
- The durable daily uses share three traits: the user already knows the command exists, it completes in one turn, and the hands/eyes are busy (car, cooking). Anything needing multi-step reliability or discovery decays.
- Reliability of the basics is a floor, not a feature: the Gemini-for-Home backlash shows that losing timers/routines costs more goodwill than new LLM abilities earn. ARES's phone should never let a "smart" path break a simple, deterministic one (timers, calls, "send me X").
- The two most valued phone-AI features in the SellCell data (Circle to Search, Writing Tools) are *in-context, one-tap* tools, not a separate assistant destination.
- LLM voice is used for long, open conversation; that is a different job from Jarvis-style delegation, and ARES needs both modes to feel distinct (quick command vs. thinking-out-loud).

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 per-assistant breakdown of daily tasks for Siri, Google Assistant/Gemini or Bixby from a primary source; the YouGov sample size/fieldwork dates were not verified.
- No public data on Galaxy AI/Bixby daily usage beyond the SellCell survey.
- No retention/abandonment curves for ChatGPT/Claude voice modes specifically.

## 2. Lessons from proactive/ambient assistants and failed AI devices

### Takeaway
Proactive features succeed when they surface one specific, verifiable fact at the exact moment it is needed (a confirmation number during an airline call, a song name, a screened spam call) and stay silent otherwise; generic daily digests (Daily Hub, early Now Brief) and confident-but-wrong summaries (Apple's news summaries) get pulled or ignored. The dedicated AI gadgets failed on latency, reliability and having no advantage over the phone.

### Cited Findings
- Pixel Magic Cue (Pixel 10, Aug 2025): surfaces info from Gmail/Calendar/Messages/screenshots inside calls and chats (e.g., flight confirmation number on the in-call screen when calling an airline; an address as a one-tap reply chip; photos when someone asks for event pictures). Google says if no appropriate action is found, it won't say anything — [Google Blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/google-pixel-magic-cue-ai-feature/), [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/08/20/pixel-10-magic-cue-launch/)
- Reviews of Magic Cue: Android Authority's reviewer found it refused to show information it had during calls and gave incorrect/unhelpful text suggestions even in staged scenarios, then later found it appeared unprompted with genuinely helpful info; MobileSyrup after months said it "rarely showed up" and the first genuinely useful suggestion (a song name in Spotify) was "massively underwhelming" — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/two-months-later-pixel-10-magic-cue-delivered-3610389/), [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/01/07/whoa-my-pixels-magic-cue-finally-did-something-useful/)
- Pixel Daily Hub: Google paused the public preview in Sept 2025, weeks after launch, "to enhance its performance and refine the personalized experience"; in testing it rarely showed anything beyond weather, suggested videos and AI prompts, couldn't tell personal from shared calendars, and was hard to find — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/pixel-10-daily-hub-pause-3595993), [Droid-Life](https://www.droid-life.com/2025/09/09/oh-google-apparently-pulled-daily-hub-on-the-pixel-10-series/), [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/google-pixel-phones/one-of-the-google-pixel-10s-most-interesting-new-features-just-got-removed-heres-what-we-know), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/google-pixel-phones/google-has-temporarily-pulled-the-pixel-10s-daily-hub-feature-in-an-attempt-to-make-it-more-useful)
- Samsung Now Brief (One UI 7, S25): lock-screen/“Now Bar” briefings from Calendar, Health, travel, routines and weather; reception mixed: SammyFans called it a useful addition "but it isn't very useful"; Xataka's writer said it didn't impress at first but grew on him as the phone learned his habits; Tom's Guide credits its design polish over Daily Hub — [SammyFans](https://www.sammyfans.com/2025/07/03/samsung-one-uis-now-brief-is-a-smart-and-useful-feature-check-eligible-devices-and-future-expansion/), [Xataka Android](https://www.xatakandroid.com/aplicaciones-android/now-brief-gran-estrella-one-ui-todas-formas-que-le-estoy-dando-uso-mi-samsung-galaxy), [SamMobile](https://www.sammobile.com/?p=16600677)
- Apple notification summaries: Apple suspended summaries for News & Entertainment apps in the iOS 18.3 beta (Jan 16, 2025) after the BBC complained (a summary falsely said Luigi Mangione had shot himself; NYT, Sky, Telegraph also affected); remaining summaries were italicised, with a beta label and per-app disabling from the lock screen — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2025/01/16/apple-temporarily-walks-back-notification-summaries/), [Silicon Republic](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/business/apple-halt-ai-notification-summaries-backlash-features)
- Despite that, notification summaries were the second most valued Apple Intelligence feature (54%) in SellCell's survey — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/12/16/most-iphone-owners-see-little-to-no-value-in-apple-intelligence-so-far/)
- Siri personal context: Apple's personal-context/on-screen-awareness Siri slipped from iOS 18 to iOS 26.4 and then to iOS 27 (Sept 2026, beta, English only, server features subject to daily usage limits); reported causes in internal testing were slow responses and accuracy problems — [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3058849/apple-just-cant-get-siri-working-right.html), [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/08/ios-27-and-siri-ai-release-date/), [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/apple-delays-gemini-powered-siri-features-ios-26-4-ios-27/) (secondary, Gurman-sourced)
- Call Screen (Pixel, since 2018): Google said it screens ~37M calls/month in the US, Canada and Japan (Pixel 6-era vendor figure); automatic mode hangs up on detected spam, otherwise rings and shows a transcript of the caller's reply; Pixel 7+ lets users choose protection levels (spam, faked numbers, first-time callers) — [Google support](https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/9118387?hl=en-GB), [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/pixel-call-screen-new-settings_id147759), [PhoneArena expansion](https://www.phonearena.com/news/google-call-screen-feature-coming-to-more-markets_id135998)
- Nothing Essential Space (Phone 3a, 2025): one press of the Essential Key = screenshot capture, long press = voice memo, both analysed in the cloud into reminders/bullets/next steps; reviewers found the capture-and-recall useful as a replacement for other organiser apps, but criticised the non-remappable key and monthly usage limits — [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/nothing-essential-space-button-3538866), [Mobility Arena](https://mobilityarena.com/ai-features-in-nothing-phone-3a-essential-space/), [TuttoAndroid](https://www.tuttoandroid.net/news/2025/04/07/nothing-essential-space-limiti-1088112/)
- ChatGPT Pulse (Sept 25, 2025, Pro, mobile first): runs overnight over chats, memory and opt-in Gmail/Calendar connectors; delivers 5–10 cited cards each morning; user can steer ("more/less of this"); OpenAI warns it may surface topics already handled — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/openai-launches-chatgpt-pulse-to-proactively-write-you-morning-briefs); no independent usefulness/retention data found
- Google Now (historical, 2012–2016+): the proactive cards were renamed to "Feed", the Now brand dropped in 2016, the successor Assistant "Snapshot" was removed ("users would be relegated to asking Assistant for things by voice instead"), and the Now Launcher stopped working in 2023; no official post-mortem exists — [Chrome Unboxed](https://chromeunboxed.com/goodbye-google-assistant-snapshot-final), [Ubergizmo](https://www.ubergizmo.com/2016/09/google-now-brand-killed-off/), [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2023/04/google-now-launcher-getting-discontinued-10-years-after-launch.html)
- Humane AI Pin (Apr 2024): The Verge's David Pierce said "at least half" the time he got no answer, a simple weather question could take ~10 s, calls often didn't connect, and the only thing he could rely on was the time; MKBHD called it "one of the worst new products I've ever reviewed"; Engadget: it "solves nothing"; $699 + $24/month; HP bought Humane's assets for $116M in Feb 2025 and the Pin was bricked on Feb 28, 2025 — [Gizmodo roundup](https://gizmodo.com/review-roundup-humane-pin-1851403020), [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/?p=853041), [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/with-the-humane-ai-pin-now-dead-what-does-the-rabbit-r1-need-to-do-to-survive)
- Rabbit R1 (Apr–May 2024): the "Large Action Model" app integrations (Uber, DoorDash) worked "only with a healthy dose of luck"; Gizmodo's Uber pickup/drop-off were wrong on first try; requests acknowledged but not executed; MKBHD called it "barely reviewable"; founder reportedly cited ~5,000 daily active users (secondhand) — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/rabbit-r1-review-ai-companion-performance-1851452097), [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/rabbit-r1-real-world-review-3440725/), [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/rabbit-r1), [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91118796/humane-ai-pin-rabbit-r1-reviews)
- Common analysis: both devices lacked a reason to exist beside a smartphone that already had a capable assistant; GPT-4o's phone voice mode (May 2024) was seen as undercutting them — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/chatgpt/gpt-4o-just-killed-the-rabbit-r1-and-humane-ai-pin-is-the-ai-device-dead)

### Inferences
- The proactive winners are narrow and moment-bound (Call Screen, Now Playing-style, Magic Cue's confirmation number). Daily digests fail when they contain generic filler (weather + videos) rather than the user's own unresolved items. ARES's morning brief should only contain things specific to the owner (overnight job results, approvals waiting, today's meetings with prep, messages needing a reply), and should be empty-tolerant.
- "Silent unless sure" (Magic Cue's rule) reduces annoyance but also makes a feature invisible; reviewers then think it doesn't work. ARES should log quiet inferences somewhere glanceable (e.g., the cards page) so the owner sees it is working without being interrupted.
- Summaries that change meaning (Apple/BBC) are the most damaging failure; ARES summaries of messages/notifications should keep the original one tap away and quote names, numbers and times verbatim.
- ARES already avoids the AI gadget trap (it is on the phone, with a real computer behind it). The gadgets' lesson for ARES is latency and "acknowledged but not executed": every request needs a visible state (heard → working → done/failed) and a result that comes back.
- Capture is an underrated proactive primitive: Essential Space's "press to save this screen/thought, let AI turn it into a reminder" maps directly onto a side-button/bubble action in ARES.

### Gaps
- No usage/retention numbers for Magic Cue, Now Brief, Daily Hub, Essential Space or Pulse.
- No recent Call Screen volume figure (latest found is Pixel 6-era).
- No official Google post-mortem on Google Now.
- Pixel "At a Glance" and "Now Playing" were not researched directly in this pass.

## 3. What makes proactivity welcome vs annoying

### Takeaway
Proactivity helps when suggestions are relevant to the current task, easy to evaluate and apply, timed to natural breakpoints, and rare; frequency is the main annoyance driver, and even useful content disrupts if delivered at the wrong moment. Classic mixed-initiative and calm-technology principles (act only when expected value beats doing nothing; use the periphery; let the user control frequency) still hold.

### Cited Findings
- CHI 2025 "Need Help? Designing Proactive AI Assistants for Programming" (Chen et al., 65 participants, GPT-4o): proactive variants raised productivity (tests passed) over a non-proactive chat baseline by 12.1% (Suggest + Preview), 18% (Suggest) and 11.6% (Persistent Suggest) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04596v2)
- Same study: preference for proactive over baseline was 90% (Suggest) and 80% (Suggest + Preview) but only 47% for "Persistent Suggest" (5 s wait instead of 20 s, 5 suggestions instead of 3); participants called it "distracting" and "annoying"; "the non-proactive chat assistant was best because it didn't interrupt what I was doing"; authors: frequency matters more than volume — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04596v2)
- Same study's design principles: one-line summary with details on expansion; one-tap apply with preview; contextual relevance; implicit feedback rather than explicit accept/dismiss controls (which got mixed reactions); time by context (wait ~5 s after an idle pause, cap ~1 per 20 s during planning, hold while user is actively working, show help right after an error); let users control proactivity (opt-in/adjustable frequency) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04596v2)
- Mehrotra et al., CHI 2016 (historical; 10,372 notifications, 474 ESM responses, 20 users): response time and perceived disruption depend on presentation, alert type, sender relationship, and the type/completion/complexity of the current task; important or useful content still causes disruption if badly timed — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1502364/1/CHI16.pdf)
- Stach et al., Sensors 2024 (~10M notifications, 922 users): notifications were least frequent 8–11 pm and that window had the lowest interaction delays; some apps must notify immediately, others can defer to opportune moments — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11053777)
- Related work cited there: shorter response delays on weekends and after 12 pm; interruptibility predictors typically reach only 70–80% accuracy — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11053777)
- Horvitz, "Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces" (CHI 1999, historical but foundational): act automatically only when expected utility exceeds doing nothing; consider uncertainty about goals; scale the precision of the service to that uncertainty; time services to the user's attention; use dialogue to resolve key uncertainties; let users invoke/stop services directly; minimise the cost of poor guesses; named failure causes: poor guessing of goals, ignoring costs/benefits of automated action, poor timing, too little user refinement — [Horvitz](https://erichorvitz.com/uiact.htm), [PDF](https://www.erichorvitz.com/chi99horvitz.pdf)
- Calm technology (Weiser & Brown; Amber Case's principles): require the smallest possible amount of attention; inform and create calm; make use of the periphery; communicate without necessarily speaking; work even when it fails; respect social norms — [Calm Tech Institute](https://calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles), [O'Reilly](https://oreilly.com/content/principles-calm-technology-we-are-not-bad-at-technology-technology-is-bad-at-us)
- Product practice: Magic Cue stays silent when it has no appropriate action — [Google Blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/google-pixel-magic-cue-ai-feature/); Pulse lets users steer topics and admits misses — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/openai-launches-chatgpt-pulse-to-proactively-write-you-morning-briefs); Apple added per-app disabling of summaries from the lock screen after the BBC errors — [Silicon Republic](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/business/apple-halt-ai-notification-summaries-backlash-features)

### Inferences
- A workable ARES rule set: (1) interrupt (sound/haptic/voice) only for time-critical items or ones the owner asked to be told about; (2) everything else goes to the periphery (reactor ring, cards, the morning brief); (3) batch non-urgent items to natural breakpoints (unlock after a gap, end of a meeting, leaving the car, evening); (4) cap unsolicited spoken interruptions per hour; (5) every proactive card has a one-tap act and a "less of this" that tunes future frequency implicitly.
- ARES's existing modes (morning/meeting/night/at the Mac) are exactly the context signals the research says matter; they should gate proactivity level, not only visuals.
- Given 70–80% interruptibility prediction accuracy, ARES should prefer deferring to interrupting when unsure, the cost of a late non-urgent card being lower than an untimely voice interruption.

### Gaps
- No found study measuring proactive *voice* (spoken) interruptions on phones/earbuds specifically; the CHI 2025 evidence is from a desktop programming task.
- No public acceptance/dismissal rates for commercial proactive features.

## 4. The "I don't want to think about what it can do" problem: discoverability and the gulf of envisioning

### Takeaway
Users of conversational assistants under-use them because they don't know what is possible, how to phrase it, or how to judge the result (the "gulf of envisioning"); breadth of use shrinks over time without help. What works is surfacing capabilities in context (chips/offers tied to what's on screen or just happened), learning from repeated behaviour, and progressive disclosure, rather than help menus or command lists.

### Cited Findings
- Subramonyam et al., CHI 2024, "Bridging the Gulf of Envisioning": the gap between intent and a prompt has three parts: knowing whether the LLM can do the task, how to instruct it, and how to evaluate whether the output meets the goal; builds on Norman's gulfs of execution/evaluation — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14459v1)
- Feedforward in generative AI (2025): frames remedies as showing users, before they act, what the system will do with an input — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14229v2)
- Smart-speaker interview study (2023, 15 users): 9 of 15 struggled with Alexa discoverability, feeling they used only basic functions while knowing many skills existed; many didn't know Alexa apps were called "skills"; authors: undiscovered features lead to under-use or abandonment — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05741)
- CHI 2020 "Alexa is a Toy": difficulty finding valuable uses was a main reason older adults abandoned Echo — [IU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/10aaf444-3a20-4708-9e07-08064c84b137)
- "What Can I Say?" (Kirschthaler, Porcheron, Fischer): compared automatically appearing prompts vs user-requested help in a voice ordering task (venue not confirmed in this pass) — [Nottingham repository](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/4632840/what-can-i-say-effects-of-discoverability-in-vuis-on-task-performance-and-user-experience)
- Furqan, Myers & Zhu, CHI EA 2017 (historical): DiscoverCal used adaptive discovery tools to improve VUI learnability; low discoverability leads to learnability problems — [Drexel](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/conferenceProceeding/Learnability-through-Adaptive-Discovery-Tools-in/991019173777804721)
- Daily Hub's failure was partly discoverability: "finding Daily Hub was more of a challenge than it needed to be" — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/google-pixel-phones/one-of-the-google-pixel-10s-most-interesting-new-features-just-got-removed-heres-what-we-know)
- Contextual one-tap offers are the pattern of the most valued phone-AI features: Circle to Search 82.1%, Writing Tools 72% (SellCell) — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/12/16/most-iphone-owners-see-little-to-no-value-in-apple-intelligence-so-far/); Magic Cue's suggestion chips in Messages — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/08/20/pixel-10-magic-cue-launch/)
- CHI 2025 proactive study: suggestions with a one-line summary, expandable details and a preview were evaluated and used more efficiently — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04596v2)
- Pulse and Xataka's Now Brief account both describe value growing as the system learns the user's habits — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/openai-launches-chatgpt-pulse-to-proactively-write-you-morning-briefs), [Xataka Android](https://www.xatakandroid.com/aplicaciones-android/now-brief-gran-estrella-one-ui-todas-formas-que-le-estoy-dando-uso-mi-samsung-galaxy)

### Inferences
- For ARES the three envisioning gaps map to: (1) "can it?" → contextual offers ("This is the PDF from your Mac's Downloads — summarise / send to …?") and the Mac's state visible from the phone; (2) "how do I say it?" → accept loose phrasing, offer 2–3 suggestion chips after each result ("send it to me", "keep watching", "do the same tomorrow"); (3) "is it right?" → results returned as artifacts (file, diff, screenshot, link), not just a spoken claim.
- "Suggest the next step after a result" is the cheapest discoverability channel: the user is already engaged and the context is exact.
- ARES already has an "observe repeated request → offer a routine once" mechanism on the Mac (autonomy.js suggestions); the research supports extending it to the phone as a primary discoverability path (offer automations from observed behaviour, once, with an easy no).
- Avoid a feature catalogue as the main discoverability path; the smart-speaker data shows lists of skills don't get used.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found on suggestion-chip uptake in LLM voice assistants on phones.
- The "What Can I Say?" results (which strategy won) were not retrieved.

## 5. What a Jarvis-like experience requires in practice

### Takeaway
The pieces the big vendors are still struggling to ship are personal context/memory (Siri slipped two years over accuracy and latency), reliable multi-step execution (Rabbit, Gemini for Home), fast turn-taking (humans expect ~200 ms gaps; vendors target 0.6–1.2 s), and a trust model where the user co-plans and approves irreversible actions. Delegation works best when the plan is visible and editable, high-stakes actions are gated, and results come back as something checkable.

### Cited Findings
- Personal context is hard even for Apple: personal-data/on-screen Siri features were delayed past iOS 26.4 to iOS 27 because of slow responses and accuracy issues in testing; iOS 27 Siri ships in beta with daily limits on server features — [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3058849/apple-just-cant-get-siri-working-right.html), [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/08/ios-27-and-siri-ai-release-date/)
- Magic Cue shows memory value lies in retrieving specific personal facts at the right time (confirmation numbers, addresses, photos) — [Google Blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/google-pixel-magic-cue-ai-feature/)
- Asynchronous work with results returned: ChatGPT Pulse researches overnight and returns cited cards in the morning — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/openai-launches-chatgpt-pulse-to-proactively-write-you-morning-briefs)
- Magentic-UI (Microsoft Research, July 2025): human-in-the-loop agent with co-planning (plan editor before execution), co-tasking, action guards (approval before potentially irreversible actions, user sets how often approvals are needed), multi-tasking and long-term memory; in user studies participants valued co-planning because plans are subjective, many edited plans to add preferences, were frustrated when the agent didn't adapt mid-task, and asked for a third option at action guards and more visibility into plan changes — [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/magentic-ui-an-experimental-human-centered-web-agent), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.22358); a "71% accuracy improvement with human guidance" figure is from a third-party summary and was not verified
- Horvitz (historical): "the final action should not be taken without user approval" where uncertainty is high; keep the cost of poor guesses low; let users stop services directly — [Horvitz](https://erichorvitz.com/uiact.htm)
- Latency: human conversational turn gaps cluster around ~200 ms across 10 languages (Stivers et al., PNAS 2009, historical); speakers need ≥600 ms to prepare a word, so people plan replies while listening — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/pdf), [EMCA wiki](https://emcawiki.net/Stivers-etal2009)
- Voice-agent latency targets (vendor heuristics, not standards): ~600–1,200 ms for tool-free turns; <800 ms feels smooth, >1,500 ms a noticeable pause; streaming reduces 2–5 s sequential pipelines to ~500–800 ms; watch tail latency — [Twig](https://www.twig.so/blog/voice-ai-agents-latency-budget-800ms), [DeepLearning.AI course](https://learn.deeplearning.ai/courses/building-ai-voice-agents-for-production/lesson/idsit/voice-agent-overview), [Famulor](https://www.famulor.io/blog/ai-voice-agent-latency-how-fast-your-phone-bot-must-reply)
- Failure benchmark: Humane's ~10 s weather answers and "no answer at least half the time" were central to its reviews — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/review-roundup-humane-pin-1851403020)
- Reliability of execution: Rabbit's integrations acknowledged requests but didn't execute them; Gemini for Home handled multi-part commands correctly about half the time — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/rabbit-r1-review-ai-companion-performance-1851452097), [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-for-google-home-impressions-3633043)
- Privacy/trust trade-off of proactive memory: "truly useful proactive AI needs access, memory, and judgment," raising privacy and control questions; Pulse connectors are opt-in and revocable — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/openai-chatgpt-pulse-pro-daily-briefings), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/openai-launches-chatgpt-pulse-to-proactively-write-you-morning-briefs)

### Inferences
- Two latency budgets: conversational acknowledgement (<1 s: a local "on it" earcon/phrase, the reactor reacting) and task completion (seconds to hours: tracked as a job with a push when done). Long tasks are fine if the acknowledgement is instant and the result is pushed back; Humane failed on the first budget.
- ARES's tier model (1 acts, 2 owner/logged, 3 explicit yes) matches the research; the Magentic-UI findings add: show the plan before multi-step work, allow editing it by voice, offer a third choice at approvals ("yes / no / change this"), and show plan changes mid-run.
- Memory should be retrieval of specific facts ("the confirmation number", "the file I edited yesterday"), and the continuity that matters most is "pick up where I left off at the Mac": the last active project, file, browser tab, build.
- Reliability on a small set of tasks beats breadth; each new phone capability should be tested end to end, including the result coming back.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study linking specific agent latencies to user satisfaction or abandonment.
- No user research found on cross-device continuity (phone ↔ desktop agent) specifically.
- No data on approval fatigue rates (how often users rubber-stamp gates).

## 6. Concrete high-value use cases for an assistant that controls the owner's computer remotely

### Takeaway
Direct evidence for "remote-control-my-computer from my phone" use is scarce; the strongest evidence-backed patterns are hands-busy/driving communication (calls, texts, reading/replying), retrieving one specific personal fact or file at the moment of need, capture-now-process-later, screening/handling calls, overnight or background work with results pushed back, and summaries of what happened while away (with originals one tap away).

### Cited Findings
- Driving is the dominant context for phone voice use (62% car use for Siri in 2016; 62% of US smartphone users use voice assistants while driving, secondary stat) — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2016/06/06/siri-embarrassment-poll/), [DemandSage](https://www.demandsage.com/voice-search-statistics/)
- Hands-free calls (39%) and timers (40%) remain core daily uses — [Implicator.ai/YouGov](https://www.implicator.ai/weather-music-timers-americans-still-use-assistants-like-its-2018.md)
- Retrieval of specific personal info mid-conversation (flight confirmation from email during a call; an address as a reply; event photos) is Google's flagship proactive use — [Google Blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/google-pixel-magic-cue-ai-feature/)
- Call screening is one of the longest-lived successful assistant features (since 2018; ~37M calls/month claimed by Google in the Pixel 6 era) — [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/google-call-screen-feature-coming-to-more-markets_id135998), [Google support](https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/9118387?hl=en-GB)
- Capture-and-organise (screenshot/voice memo → reminders, next steps) was found genuinely useful in Essential Space reviews — [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/nothing-essential-space-button-3538866)
- Notification summaries are valued (54% of Apple Intelligence users) but error-prone for news — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/12/16/most-iphone-owners-see-little-to-no-value-in-apple-intelligence-so-far/), [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2025/01/16/apple-temporarily-walks-back-notification-summaries/)
- Background research/briefings returned as cards (Pulse) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/25/openai-launches-chatgpt-pulse-to-proactively-write-you-morning-briefs)
- Writing help is the most valued Apple Intelligence feature (72%) and writing dominates ChatGPT work use — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/12/16/most-iphone-owners-see-little-to-no-value-in-apple-intelligence-so-far/), [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255)
- Proactive help right after an error was one of the most accepted suggestion types (latent-error debugging and brainstorming accepted most often) in the CHI 2025 study — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04596v2)

### Inferences (ARES-specific, derived from the findings above; not directly evidenced)
- Highest-value candidates, ordered by evidence strength:
  1. "Send me that file / the latest X": fuzzy retrieval by recency, project or app ("the deck I edited this afternoon"), result as a file card on the phone. Analogue of Magic Cue's retrieval, with the Mac as the source.
  2. "What did my build/job/Claude session do?" with a push on finish or failure, a one-paragraph result, and chips (retry / show diff / open on Mac later). Errors are the moment proactive help is most accepted.
  3. Draft-then-confirm messaging and email by voice (writing is the top-valued AI feature); read back, then tier-3 send. In the car: read incoming messages, reply by voice, summarise threads.
  4. "While you were away" digests at breakpoints (unlocking after a gap, leaving a meeting, getting in the car): jobs finished, approvals pending, important messages, with originals one tap away and names/numbers quoted verbatim.
  5. Location/time-triggered follow-ups ("remind me when I get home", "when I'm back at the Mac open the PR"), linking the phone's context to Mac actions.
  6. Capture-now, process-later: side button/bubble press saves the current screen or a voice note; ARES files it, makes a task, or starts a Mac job.
  7. Call handling: screen unknown callers, take a message, answer with a quick text; in a meeting mode, decline with a summary.
  8. Overnight/background work with a morning card (research, project progress, routine results), containing only the owner's own items.
- Each use case needs the full loop to count: instant acknowledgement, visible progress, a result on the phone, and a next-step chip.

### Gaps
- No usage data found for remote-desktop-via-assistant or "phone as remote for a desktop agent" products (e.g., Claude Code remote control, ChatGPT agent on mobile, Microsoft Phone Link with Copilot); a targeted search could fill this.
- No sourced evidence on location-triggered reminder usage rates in 2023–2026.
- Galaxy Buds-specific assistant usage (e.g., Bixby/Gemini via earbuds, reading notifications aloud) was not researched in this pass.
