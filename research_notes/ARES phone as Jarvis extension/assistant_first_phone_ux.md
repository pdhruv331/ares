# Assistant-first phone UX: launchers, invocation, glanceable/generative UI, voice, and agent trust (2023–2026)

Scope note: researched 2026-10-10 with web search plus two full-page fetches (Android Live Updates design guide, Google Research generative UI post). About 30 tool calls. Most product coverage is 2025–2026 launch and review reporting. Where a claim comes only from a search-engine summary of a page, not from reading the page, it is marked "(summary)". Anything from the model's own background knowledge that I could not confirm has been moved to Gaps. ARES-specific recommendations are in the Inferences subsections because the template has no separate section for them.

## 1. AI/assistant-first launchers and home screens: what works, what reviewers criticise

### Takeaway
The assistant-first home screens that reviewers praise share three traits. They capture things with one gesture (Nothing's Essential Key), they show live, time-bound status in a single system strip (Samsung Now Bar, Android 16 Live Updates, iOS Live Activities), and they stay minimal with fast one-thumb access to everything else (Niagara). The products that failed (Humane AI Pin, Rabbit R1 at launch) replaced the phone's visible affordances with an opaque voice-first box that did few things slowly. Proactive AI home features such as Pixel Magic Cue and Now Brief get criticised mainly when they are unreliable or show up rarely, not for the idea itself.

### Cited Findings
**Nothing (Essential Space, Essential Key, Phone (3))**
- Android Authority found Essential Space useful, calling it the best of Pixel Screenshots, Recorder and a notes app rolled into one. Its core flow is capturing screenshots or voice memos, which the app analyses in the cloud to pull out reminders and bullet points. The reviewer argued the dedicated hardware button wasn't needed (summary). — [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/nothing-essential-space-button-3538866)
- The Essential Key takes a screenshot on a press and records a voice memo on a hold, and both go into Essential Space. Nothing gives no option to remap it, and users have worked around this with ADB and Key Mapper (summary). — [Beebom](https://beebom.com/nothing-essential-key-user-remaps-button-with-adb/amp/); [nothingtec](https://nothingtec.com/?p=3731)
- Phone (3) reviews split on the rear Glyph Matrix (a small round LED dot-matrix display running a clock, battery and "Glyph Toys"). Essential Space got more praise, but one review said the AI button "still doesn't have too many use cases" (summary). — [Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/nothing-phone-3-review-pros-con/); [ChannelNews](https://www.channelnews.com.au/review-nothing-phone-3-flashy-and-fun-but-is-it-enough/); [t2online](https://t2online.in/tech/tech-reviews/review--nothing-phone-3-has-a-polarising-design--unifying-software-experience/2000348)

**Samsung Now Bar / Now Brief (the owner's own platform)**
- The first Now Bar (One UI 7) worked only with Samsung's own apps. One UI 8 opened it to third-party apps, and Android 16 Live Updates feed into it (Google confirmed food-delivery alerts as one example). Builds showed ongoing calls in it. One UI 8.5 beta added a torch-on indicator (summary). — [Android Authority, One UI 8.5](https://www.androidauthority.com/samsung-one-ui-8-5-now-bar-now-brief-updates-3624287/); [Gizchina](https://www.gizchina.com/2025/07/13/one-ui-8-2-features-that-make-us-want-a-samsung/); [SamMobile](https://www.sammobile.com/news/we-take-a-tour-of-samsungs-latest-one-ui-8-build/)
- Early reviewers called the Now Bar well designed with potential, but "still very limited" (summary, Spanish-language review). — [Xataka Android](https://www.xatakandroid.com/sistema-operativo/one-ui-7-trajo-uno-mejores-cambios-para-samsung-galaxy-todavia-no-suficiente-esto-le-pido-a-one-ui-8)
- Now Brief in One UI 8 added auto-drafted, shareable birthday messages built from past interactions, said to be processed on-device. One test build removed its read-aloud option. One UI 8.5 moved its YouTube section to a horizontal carousel (summary). — [Tuttoandroid](https://www.tuttoandroid.net/news/2025/07/10/samsung-one-ui-8-novita-now-bar-now-brief-1104938/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/samsung-one-ui-8-5-now-bar-now-brief-updates-3624287/)

**Android 16 Live Updates (fetched official guide)**
- Definition: "Live updates provide a summary of important updates so users can track progress without opening the app." — [Android Developers: Live update notifications](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/live-updates)
- Use them for "finite or trackable experiences initiated by the user". Don't use them "if there is not a clear end time". They don't work well when information is bundled from multiple apps, when the notification is meant to give recommendations, or when it needs bespoke visuals or animations. — [same](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/live-updates)
- Alerts: "alert only for critical status changes. Don't alert for minor variable adjustments", for example "alert for driver arrived, but don't alert for a shift in ETA." Also: "If you do alert, the UI should provide immediate visual evidence of why." — [same](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/live-updates)
- Discrete steps should be clearly labelled phases, and a progress bar's fill must match the remaining time or distance. Use the same timestamp format in the status-bar chip as in the expanded card. Users can dismiss or demote a Live Update to a normal notification. — [same](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/live-updates)
- Google's guidance (summary) also excludes ads, promotions, chat messages, alerts, upcoming calendar events and "quick access to app features" from Live Updates. — [search summary of the same guide](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/live-updates)
- `Notification.ProgressStyle` marks points and segments on a progress bar (rideshare, delivery, navigation), and a progress-centric notification can be "promoted" to a Live Update with a few lines of code (summary). — [Android Developers: progress-centric notifications](https://developer.android.google.cn/about/versions/16/features/progress-centric-notifications?hl=pt-br); [Just Eat Takeaway engineering](https://medium.com/justeattakeaway-tech/live-updates-and-progress-notifications-for-android-16-at-jet-b0c87eab17b4); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/android-16-live-notifications-3518375)

**iOS Live Activities / Dynamic Island (HIG via mirrors; summary)**
- Offer a Live Activity only for tasks and events with a defined beginning and end. Don't offer one for a task that runs over eight hours, and end it right after the task completes. People expect a summary and key facts, not lots of detail or actions. Show an innocuous summary when the content is sensitive. No ads. Avoid starting one people don't expect, because surprises lead them to switch Live Activities off in Settings. Update only when there is new content and alert only when it's essential. Support all three Dynamic Island presentations (compact, minimal, expanded) plus the Lock Screen. — [Apple HIG mirror](https://developer-rno.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/components/system-experiences/live-activities); [9to5Mac 2022 (older)](https://9to5mac.com/2022/09/26/iphone-14-pro-live-activities-guidelines/); [Michael Tsai 2022 (older)](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2022/11/03/live-activity-guidelines)

**Pixel proactive layer (Magic Cue)**
- Google pitched Magic Cue as anticipating needs and suggesting information or actions from on-phone context, processed on the Tensor G5 (for example, flight details when you dial the airline). Android Authority found it either not working or showing incorrect information for about two months, including in Google's own recommended tests. MobileSyrup called it "a pretty big flop" at first, then reported in January 2026 that it finally did something useful after several months. — [Android Authority, one month](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-pixel-10-magic-cue-one-month-later-3598684); [Android Authority, two months](https://www.androidauthority.com/two-months-later-pixel-10-magic-cue-delivered-3610389); [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/01/07/whoa-my-pixels-magic-cue-finally-did-something-useful/)

**Minimalist launchers (Niagara; Olauncher thinly covered)**
- Niagara is praised for a single list with no drawer clutter and an alphabet rail reachable one-handed from the bottom half of either side. It is criticised for conflicts with the back gesture, a one-widget limit, no folders, freezes on older devices and a premium subscription (about $4 a year per one forum post). — [Android Police (2021, older)](https://www.androidpolice.com/2021/02/02/hands-on-the-minimalist-niagara-launcher-is-officially-out-of-beta/); [Kimola Play-review report](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-insights-niagara-launcher-feedback-report-google-play-en-157183); [Tildes thread](https://tildes.net/~tech/19fu/minimalist_android_launcher_suggestions)

**Rabbit R1 / Humane AI Pin (2024, the negative examples)**
- R1: touch worked only for the on-screen keyboard, navigation used a button and scroll wheel with "maddening" sensitivity, it worked with four apps at launch, and a phone did the same things faster. MKBHD called it "barely reviewable". The Verge preferred it to the Pin because "you can see the thing working so the AI delays don't feel quite so interminable." Rabbit later moved to a card-based UI resembling Apple Wallet (summary). — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/228146/rabbit-r1-ai-reviews-gadget-hardware); [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/everything-you-should-know-before-you-buy-the-rabbit-r1-1851439318); [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/ai-gadget-problems)
- Humane AI Pin: MKBHD called it the "worst product I've ever reviewed" (battery, speed, no alarm or timer). The Verge said it was "so thoroughly unfinished and so totally broken". Consumer service shut down on 28 February 2025 (summary). — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/ai-gadget-problems); [Quartz](https://qz.com/rabbit-r1-ai-companion-1851442882)

### Inferences
- The R1 versus Pin comparison and the Live Update rules point the same way: show visible, glanceable state while the AI works. ARES's reactor plus captions already does this for voice. Reviewers liked the R1 better for the same reason.
- **ARES recommendation (high value, low cost):** publish ARES jobs, projects, Mac charge/wake and calls as Android 16 `ProgressStyle` promoted Live Updates. Since One UI 8 these should reach the Samsung **Now Bar** on the lock screen and the status-bar chip without opening the launcher, which puts ARES on the surface Samsung itself promotes. Follow Google's rules: only user-initiated, finite jobs, named phases (planning → running → tests → verifying → done), alert only on state changes (needs approval, failed, done), never for progress ticks. Routines and suggestions don't qualify because they are recommendations, so they belong on page-2 cards instead.
- Essential Space suggests a capture inbox: one gesture captures the screen or a voice note, and ARES turns it into a task, reminder or job. ARES has most of the parts (orb drops on the Mac, Notification access, voice). A "capture" action on the side key's double-press, or a long-press on the reactor, that files the current screen plus a voice note to the Mac would copy the most-praised part of Nothing's design. Reviewers' complaint about Nothing (a fixed button with no remapping) argues for letting the owner remap it.
- Magic Cue's reception shows that proactive suggestions that rarely fire or are wrong erode trust faster than having none. Ship few proactive cards, with high precision.
- Niagara's praise suggests the app drawer should stay one-handed: the letter rail on the bottom half of both sides, and no conflict with the edge back gesture. CLAUDE.md already notes the left-edge back-gesture conflict on the emulator.

### Gaps
- I found no Verge or 9to5Google full review of the Nothing Phone (3) interface, no reviews of Olauncher, Before Launcher or Smart Launcher's AI features, and no hands-on review of the final One UI 8 Now Bar. Coverage is mostly beta or launch reporting.
- Pixel "At a Glance" (2023–2026) and the "intent-based home screen" concept pieces were not researched for lack of budget.
- I did not verify whether third-party promoted Live Updates appear in the Now Bar on every One UI 8 build, or whether Samsung whitelists apps. This needs a test on the owner's phone.

## 2. Invocation and low-friction input

### Takeaway
The current state of the art is several redundant invocation paths that all land in the same overlay: power or side-key long-press, hotword, back-tap, corner swipe and lock screen. The overlay already knows the screen ("Circle anything or ask about this screen"). On Android, a third-party app gets the side key and assist gesture only by holding the system ASSISTANT role, and that role is reported to reset on every APK reinstall.

### Cited Findings
- Samsung Galaxy: side-button long-press launches Gemini by default and can be changed under Settings > Advanced features > Side button > Long press. Double-press and long-press can be assigned to apps or in-app features, varying by model and region (summary). — [Samsung support](https://www.samsung.com/uk/support/mobile-devices/how-to-customise-the-side-button-with-new-features-on-your-galaxy-phone-and-tablet/)
- Gemini overlay entry points are the "Hey Google" hotword, long-pressing power, and a diagonal swipe from a bottom corner. The upgraded overlay shows "Circle anything or ask about this screen". A selected area attaches as an image, and the plus menu has a "Screen content" option. A teardown suggests Gemini may decide on its own when a question is about the screen (summary). — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/08/25/gemini-overlay-pill/); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/gemini-overlay-adding-circle-to-search-more-broadly-3665864/); [Android Authority, "Tell me about this"](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-tell-me-about-this-3577321)
- Pixel Quick Tap (double-tap the back) can open the digital assistant (Settings > System > Gestures > Quick Tap). A December 2025 update made it more consistent, and 2026 user reports say it sometimes stops responding (summary, the second source is weaker). — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/how-customize-back-tap-gestures-google-pixel-phone); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-pixel-quick-tap-gesture-improvement-3622638); [itechify](https://itechify.com/2026/08/21/pixel-gesture-not-working-2026/)
- Gemini on the lock screen: the "Use Gemini without unlocking" toggle allows general questions while locked. Personal content such as the calendar requires an unlock. A separate toggle allows calls and messages without unlocking (summary). — [Google Gemini Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14576209); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/02/03/gemini-extensions-android-lockscreen/)
- Android's assistant role: the selected voice-interaction app is the holder of RoleManager's ASSISTANT role, and its VoiceInteractionService is bound by the system at boot and always running. Starting a session from system UI uses a hidden API (AssistUtils) that only bundled apps can call, so a third-party app has to be the selected assistant (Automotive docs, summary). — [AOSP: voice interaction integration flows](https://source.android.com/docs/automotive/voice/voice_interaction_guide/integration_flows)
- A developer report says the `assistant` and `voice_interaction_service` secure settings are cleared on every `adb install -r` for an app holding ROLE_ASSISTANT (including the official Claude app), which breaks power-button long-press. The workaround is `adb shell settings put secure …` after each install (summary, single report). — [claude-code issue #41696](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/41696); [mirror](https://claudeissues.com/issue/41696-android-role-assistant-settings-cleared-on-apk-reinstall-affects-both-custom-and)
- Nothing's Essential Key uses press for a screenshot and hold for a voice memo, with no remap (see section 1). — [Beebom](https://beebom.com/nothing-essential-key-user-remaps-button-with-adb/amp/)

### Inferences
- **ARES recommendation:** make ARES the phone's default digital assistant (ROLE_ASSISTANT plus a VoiceInteractionService) if it isn't already. CLAUDE.md mentions "side-button summon" and "assistant summons listen on the home screen", which suggests this is partly done. Every system entry point (side-key long-press, corner swipe, Quick-Tap-style gestures) then lands in ARES. Add a post-install check, or a debug-build adb step, that restores the role, because reinstalls reportedly clear it. Read the role holder on launch and show a one-tap "ARES is no longer your assistant: fix" chip.
- Copy the overlay pattern: invoking from any app should open a small ARES sheet that already holds the current screen (assist structure or screenshot, behind a toggle) with a "circle or ask about this" hint. "Look at this" is then one gesture, not a share flow.
- Lock-screen policy should match Gemini's split: general questions and Mac status while locked, personal data (calendar, messages, files from the Mac) after an unlock. Approvals for tier 3 actions should always require an unlock or biometrics (see section 6).
- Map the remaining gestures to distinct verbs, not to the same action. Side-key long-press = talk. Double-press = capture (screenshot plus voice note to ARES). Hold-to-talk on the reactor stays. Earbud long-press = talk with no screen.

### Gaps
- No measured time-to-assistant benchmarks were found for any invocation path.
- Share-sheet "send to assistant", text-selection actions (`ACTION_PROCESS_TEXT`), copy-to-assistant, squeeze (Pixel Active Edge, discontinued) and Galaxy Buds or Watch triggers were not researched with sources.
- No official developer.android.com page was fetched on how a phone (not Automotive) maps the side key to a third-party assistant. Confirm on One UI 8 that the "Digital assistant app" setting lists ARES.

## 3. Generative and adaptive UI (cards, not text)

### Takeaway
The field moved in 2025 from "the model writes HTML" (Google's generative UI in Gemini and AI Mode) to "the model emits declarative component specs that the native client renders" (Google's A2UI, December 2025). For a native Kotlin launcher, the second approach fits best: ARES's brain returns typed card specs from a fixed component catalogue, and the phone draws them with its own components and theme. Speed is the main weakness of fully generated UI (a minute or more).

### Cited Findings
- Google Research (18 November 2025, Leviathan, Valevski, Natchu, Matias), "Generative UI: LLMs are Effective UI Generators": Gemini 3 Pro builds a whole interactive experience per prompt (pages, tools, games). It uses server-side tools (image generation, search), detailed system instructions, and post-processors that fix common errors, and products can force a consistent style. — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/)
- Evaluation, with generation speed excluded: human-expert websites were preferred most, generative UI came close behind, and both led raw text or markdown and the top search result by a wide margin. The post gives no percentages. Limitations: generation "can take a minute or more", outputs occasionally contain inaccuracies, and quality depends heavily on the model. — [same](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/)
- Deployed as the Gemini app's "dynamic view" and "visual layout" experiments, and in Search AI Mode for Pro and Ultra subscribers in the US. — [same](https://research.google/blog/generative-ui-a-rich-custom-visual-interactive-user-experience-for-any-prompt/)
- Google's A2UI: agents emit a declarative data format that the client renders with its own native components, "retaining full control over styling and security". Agents pick from pre-approved component catalogues instead of shipping executable code, which avoids the heavy, visually disjointed and less secure HTML-in-iframe approach. It is transport-agnostic and often paired with CopilotKit's AG-UI. Renderers exist for React, Flutter, Angular and SwiftUI per third parties. Version numbers (v0.9.x) are unverified. — [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/); [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/ai/google-launches-a2ui-project-to-enable-agents-to-build-contextually-relevant-uis/); [Analytics Vidhya](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2025/12/google-a2ui-explained/)
- Live Updates explicitly don't suit "bespoke visuals, animations, or unique data structures", so rich generated content belongs in the app or on cards, not in system notifications. — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/live-updates)
- Rabbit's revamp to Wallet-like cards (section 1) is another product move from chat output to stacked cards. — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/ai-gadget-problems)

### Inferences
- **ARES recommendation:** define an "ARES card" schema as a small A2UI-style catalogue: header, fact rows, list, progress or phases, map pin, media, chips (`[data-act]`-style actions), confirm block (amber, tiered). Let the brain return a card alongside or instead of text, so "what's my day", "how's the build" or "find me a ride" come back as tappable cards placed under the reactor or on the board. The phone renders natively in the current `Scheme`. This reuses the Mac's existing `[data-act]` click pattern and avoids WebViews.
- Speed: fully generated UI takes about a minute, while a filled template is instant. Use fixed templates filled by the model for daily use, and keep "build me a custom panel" (already in ARES's custom panels on the Mac) as the slow path.
- Adaptive home: let modes (morning, meeting, night, at the Mac) choose which card templates appear and in what order, not a free-form generated layout. Fixed positions keep it glanceable, and Amershi-style "limit disruptive changes" applies (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Not researched in depth: ChatGPT Apps SDK and canvas, Claude artifacts on mobile, Android Jetpack Glance widget docs, and academic adaptive-UI studies. The OpenAI Apps SDK / MCP Apps parallel to A2UI is from background knowledge only and unverified here.

## 4. Discoverability without effort

### Takeaway
"Ask anything" boxes fail at discoverability because users don't know what the system can do (the "blank box" or capability-ambiguity problem). The evidence-backed fixes are suggestion chips tied to what's on screen and right now, which teach capabilities as they help. Proactive offers must be well timed, at task boundaries rather than mid-task, or they are judged disruptive even when the content is identical.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g's qualitative usability tests found most AI features failed both findability and discoverability: participants didn't expect AI features, didn't notice them, and didn't use them, often not realising they existed (secondary summaries of NN/g, original not read). — [Eleken](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/make-ai-features-discoverable); [Leon Furze, 2026](https://leonfurze.com/2026/03/18/iykyk-how-do-we-know-what-ai-can-really-do/)
- The "blank box problem": users face capability ambiguity because they don't know what the system can actually do. — [Aaron Tay](https://aarontay.substack.com/p/the-blank-box-problem-why-its-harder)
- A generic "Ask AI" label says how to start but not what kinds of request work. Contextual suggestions such as "Compare these options by warmth, weight, and waterproofing" teach that the agent can reason about what's in view. — [Algolia](https://www.algolia.com/de/blog/ux/how-ui-choices-shape-engagement-with-agentic-features)
- Enterprise assistant research (arXiv 2412.10933) proposes proactive question suggestions that guide the immediate task and teach the system's capabilities. — [arXiv 2412.10933](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10933)
- Non-experts often don't know which questions to ask to assess an AI system's capabilities and limits (KR 2022, older). — [Verma et al.](https://proceedings.kr.org/2022/36/kr2022-0036-verma-et-al.pdf)
- Proactive timing: in Pu et al.'s study of proactive AI programming support (arXiv 2502.18658, venue unverified), "temporal appropriateness" was the main factor separating helpful from disruptive help. The same suggestion was supportive at a natural pause and disruptive mid-task. Frequent suggestions risked learned helplessness. Interventions at task transitions worked more consistently than idle-time triggers (as summarised by follow-up work). — [arXiv 2502.18658](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18658); [arXiv 2602.00880](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00880)
- A CHI '25 wearable-AI paper proposes a "Goldilocks Time Window" for interventions, neither too early (false positives) nor too late. — [arXiv 2504.09332](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09332)
- Microsoft's 18 Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction (CHI 2019, older) were validated with 49 practitioners against 20 AI products and are grouped by phase: initially, during interaction, when wrong, over time. — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)
- Magic Cue (section 1) shows a proactive layer losing credibility when it rarely fires or is wrong. — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/two-months-later-pixel-10-magic-cue-delivered-3610389)

### Inferences
- **ARES recommendation, "what can I do here" chips:** under the reactor, show two or three chips that change with context: foreground notification ("summarise", "reply by voice"), mode ("morning: brief me", "meeting soon: join / notes"), Mac state ("Mac asleep: wake", "build running: status"), clipboard ("copied an address: navigate / send to Mac"). Each chip names a capability, which works as a hint rather than a blank prompt, so it answers the owner's "I have to think about what I can do".
- Learned shortcuts: ARES already has `observe`/`suggestion` (it offers a routine once for a repeated request). Do the same on the phone: after the third similar voice request, offer to pin it as a chip or card, once only, and never auto-create it. This follows the over-time guidelines.
- Timing: show proactive cards at transitions (unlock, arriving home, a meeting ending, a job finishing), never while the owner is typing in another app or mid-call. Limit them to one proactive card at a time and require that each card has an action.

### Gaps
- The original NN/g article and its study details were not read. The exact wording of Microsoft's 18 guidelines was not verified (the list used here, e.g. "limit disruptive changes", is from background knowledge). No mobile-specific study on suggestion chips' effect on capability discovery was found.

## 5. Voice in daily life

### Takeaway
Evidence, much of it older, supports the intuition that people speak when hands or eyes are busy, when the query is short or local, or when they're alone or on the move, and type in public for social and privacy reasons. Earbuds change the calculus (lower volume, more private), but I found little rigorous 2023–2026 work on whispering or earbud input. The design implication: voice should never be the only path. Every voice action needs a silent equivalent and a screen response.

### Cited Findings
- Google (Interspeech 2010, older), from a deployed spoken-search log: spoken queries were predicted by awkward keyboards, location-tied topics and topics answerable hands-free. Longer queries were more likely to be typed. — [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/say-what-why-users-choose-to-speak-their-web-queries/); [ISCA PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2010/kamvar10_interspeech.pdf)
- A St Andrews study comparing touch typing and speech on smartphones found speech more effective when convenience and constraints dominate. Typing was preferred in public because of social concerns, network latency and background noise (summary; year and venue unverified). — [St Andrews repository](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/18851)
- Moorthy and Vu (2014, older): users were more cautious about speaking private than non-private information, and this was amplified in public and with conspicuous input such as voice. — [Springer](https://www.springerprofessional.de/doi/10.1007/978-3-319-07863-2_32)
- A Voicebot survey found 46% of respondents are annoyed by others using voice commands in public (date not shown). — [Voicebot](https://voicebot.ai/?p=13499)
- Google Research's "Quick Commands" (wake-word-free earbud control) was tested with 80+ users in quiet rooms, while walking and in noise, and looked at comfort with voice during earbud wear in private versus public (date unconfirmed). — [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/sound-as-an-interface-methods-to-evaluate-voice-user-interface-vui-experiences-in-various-contexts/)
- A UbiComp 2024 preliminary field study examined acceptable timing for voice notifications through earables. — [LY Corp research](https://research.lycorp.co.jp/en/publications/2083)
- A 2009 Tellme/Microsoft-commissioned survey (much older, vendor-linked): 93% said they were comfortable using voice while walking and 71% at a restaurant. — [Microsoft News 2009](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2009/07/29/on-the-go-running-errands-or-waiting-in-line-study-shows-smartphone-users-prefer-using-their-voice-to-typing-or-touching/)
- The R1 versus Pin comparison: a screen showing the AI working makes voice latency feel shorter. — [Creative Bloq / The Verge via summary](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/ai-gadget-problems)

### Inferences
- **ARES recommendation:** keep push-to-talk (hold) as the default on the phone, since it is social, deliberate and privacy-safe, and allow hands-free only through earbuds or when the phone is docked or at home (modes). Already partly built with Buds detection.
- Always pair speech with a screen result: a caption plus a card. Short answers are spoken, long ones are spoken briefly with the card holding detail (ARES's orb on the Mac already opens the portal for answers over 320 characters, and the same rule could apply on the phone).
- In public (no earbuds, not at home), default ARES's reply to captions only, or a lower volume, and offer a typed or chip path. A "quiet" mode chip on the reactor would make the silent path obvious.
- Personal or sensitive replies (messages, finances, health) should go to captions only unless earbuds are connected. The Moorthy and Vu privacy effect supports this.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 CHI/CSCW study on whispering to phone assistants or earbuds was found. No data on push-to-talk versus hands-free preference on phones. Multimodal (voice plus screen) response studies were not located.

## 6. Trust, control and approvals for autonomous agents on mobile

### Takeaway
Shipping agents converge on the same pattern: work autonomously through reversible steps, stop at the commit point (payment, send, delete, credentials) and hand control back with a strong notification, let the user watch or take over, classify actions by risk with per-site or per-action grants, and offer an immediate stop. UX research adds that confirmations lose force when overused (people click through), so reversible actions should get undo and confirmations should be reserved for rare, irreversible ones.

### Cited Findings
- Gemini screen automation on the Galaxy S26 (March 2026): the user asks for, say, a Popeyes order on Uber Eats, and Gemini runs it in the background, fills the cart but won't finalise checkout. At that point it "send[s] a notification (including a strong vibration) and hand[s] the controls back". Progress can be reviewed in a pop-up resembling a Live Update (coverage varies). The value is "less about speed" and more about doing the grunt work while you do other things. In one 9to5Google test the full-screen preview locked the phone until a forced reboot. Launch apps were Uber, Lyft, Uber Eats, DoorDash, Grubhub and Starbucks (lists conflict), US English only. — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/03/12/gemini-android-app-automation-galaxy-s26-rollout/); [SamMobile](https://www.sammobile.com/news/galaxy-s26-gemini-task-automation-now-live/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-galaxy-s26-pixel-10-control-other-apps-3643939/); [Croma](https://www.croma.com/unboxed/gemini-learns-to-use-android-apps-on-samsung-s26-phones-to-order-food-rides-and-more)
- A merchant-side analysis of Gemini Intelligence: the flow pauses before payment and personal-data entry, and the sign-off is an on-screen confirmation, not a signed credential (secondary). — [Stellagent](https://stellagent.ai/insights/samsung-galaxy-gemini-agentic-execution)
- A Gemini-in-Gmail teardown: clarifying questions, then a structured draft, then an explicit Send. It recommends never auto-sending. — [AI UX Playground](https://aiuxplayground.com/teardowns/gemini/human-loop)
- ChatGPT agent safeguards: user confirmations for high-impact actions, refusals, prompt-injection monitoring and "watch mode" requiring supervision on certain sites. Takeover mode is the recommended route for typing passwords or private information. Users are told to enable only the apps a task needs and to stop tasks immediately if something looks suspicious. The example risk is a malicious comment telling the agent to fetch a Gmail reset code. — [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11752874-chatgpt-agent)
- Claude in Chrome permission modes: "Manually approve" (formerly "Ask before acting"), "Automatically approve" (Claude reviews each action, blocks unsafe ones and asks when needed), and "Skip all approvals" (formerly "Act without asking"). Some sites require approval for every action, "Allow this action" grants one action only, and Claude asks before accessing financial sites. Blocked outright: purchases, account creation, bypassing bot checks, trades, permanent deletion. The docs are inconsistent on whether purchases are blocked or need asking. Prompt-injection success was cut from 23.6% to 11.2% at launch, and a later Anthropic figure of under 0.08% comes from internal testing (vendor claim). — [Claude Help: permissions](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12902446-claude-in-chrome-permissions-guide); [Anthropic: Claude for Chrome](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-chrome); [Claude Help: using safely](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12902428-using-claude-for-chrome-safely.)
- Microsoft Research Magentic-UI (2025): co-planning lets the user edit the plan (editor or text) before any action. Action guards ask approval before potentially irreversible actions, with user-set frequency. The system is sandboxed. Six mechanisms: co-planning, co-tasking, multi-tasking, action guards, long-term memory (and others). Evaluated on benchmarks, with simulated users, in qualitative studies and in safety tests. — [Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/magentic-ui-an-experimental-human-centered-web-agent); [arXiv 2507.22358](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22358v1)
- Confirmation versus undo: Nielsen's "Confirmation Dialogs Can Prevent User Errors — If Not Overused". People who are asked "Are you sure?" for everything stop reading and click OK. Undo with a visible restore notification is preferred for reversible actions, and specific confirmation copy for irreversible ones (secondary summaries). — [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/confirmation-dialogs-how-to-design-dialogues-without-irritation-7b4cf2599956); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/137foundry/how-to-choose-between-confirmation-dialogs-undo-windows-and-soft-delete-patterns-1opj); [U Calgary 1997 (older)](https://grouplab.cpsc.ucalgary.ca/saul/681/1997/kevin/preverr.html)
- The Live Update alert rule ("alert only for critical status changes") applies to agent progress notifications. — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/home-screen/live-updates)

### Inferences
- ARES already has the architecture the industry converged on: tiers 1/2/3, an audit log, a STOP ALL kill switch, amber approval boxes, and `start_project` read-back. The phone gaps are presentation and friction:
  - **Approval as an actionable Live Update or notification:** a tier-3 request should arrive as a high-priority notification with a one-line, plain-language preview of the action, its target and its consequence ("Send 'Running 10 min late' to Mom on WhatsApp"). Approve should require biometrics or an unlock. Deny and "Show details" stay. Use a strong vibration only for "needs you", as Gemini does at checkout.
  - **Preview before act:** for multi-step jobs, show the plan as editable steps (Magentic-UI co-planning) in the WORK card before tier-2/3 execution, with skip and edit per step.
  - **Undo over confirm for reversible tier-1/2 actions:** act immediately and show a 10-second "Undo" snackbar or notification action (calendar add, reminder, moving a file into a Mac folder). Keep confirmations rare so they keep their weight.
  - **Watch or take over:** while a Mac UI-automation step runs, the phone's Live Update could offer "Watch" (a stream of the highlighted step plus a screenshot) and "Take over / Stop", in line with ChatGPT's watch and takeover modes.
  - **Per-domain grants:** offer "allow this kind of action for this app or contact for 24 h" after repeated approvals, like Claude in Chrome's per-site grants, but always one-time for money, credentials and deletion.
  - **Activity log glanceability:** a "What ARES did today" card (from `audit.jsonl`) with an undo where possible. Trust calibration depends on seeing outcomes, not only requests.
  - **Kill switch reach:** STOP ALL should be reachable from the lock screen (notification action plus a long-press on the reactor) without unlocking. Stopping is always safe, so it needs no authentication.
- Gemini's forced-reboot bug suggests that any full-screen "agent is driving" view must keep a guaranteed escape (back or home always works).

### Gaps
- Banking-style approval UX (out-of-band push approvals, number matching) was not researched with sources. Classic trust-in-automation theory (Lee & See 2004, calibrated trust) and 2023–2026 CHI/CSCW studies on agent oversight on mobile were not located in this pass. No measured data on approval fatigue rates for AI agents was found.
- OpenAI Operator-specific (pre-merger) confirmation behaviour and the ChatGPT agent system card were not read directly.
