# Fluid UI and Motion for the ARES Android Launcher (Views + Canvas), October 2026

Scope note: these notes cover what makes a phone UI feel fluid and how to get there in a classic-Views Kotlin launcher (compileSdk/targetSdk 36, Samsung One UI 8). Where a claim comes from my own API knowledge and was not re-verified against a fetched page in this session, it is placed under "Inferences" and labelled "(API knowledge, unverified here)". Several primary pages (m3.material.io) are JavaScript-only and could not be fetched; the M3 Expressive spring numbers below come from the Compose Material 3 source code instead, which is the authoritative implementation.

## 1. Design language references (2025-2026): M3 Expressive, One UI 8, iOS 26 Liquid Glass, Nothing OS, Pixel launcher; what makes them feel fluid

### Takeaway
Every current reference system has converged on the same recipe: spring physics instead of fixed durations, motion that starts from the current on-screen value and inherits finger velocity, symmetric spatial paths, and light/material effects (glow, glass, morphing shapes) layered on top. Reviewer reaction shows the recipe is fragile: One UI 8 and Liquid Glass both drew complaints that animations were "too fast", "rough" or "too much", so tuning and restraint matter as much as the technique.

### Cited Findings
- M3 Expressive's motion tokens (as shipped in Compose Material 3 `ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt`) are six springs, Spatial (position/size) and Effects (opacity/colour) in Fast/Default/Slow: DefaultSpatial damping 0.8 / stiffness 380; FastSpatial 0.6 / 800; SlowSpatial 0.8 / 200; DefaultEffects 1.0 / 1600; FastEffects 1.0 / 3800; SlowEffects 1.0 / 800 — [AOSP androidx ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/support/+/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt)
- Third-party ports describe the "standard" (non-expressive) scheme as more damped (e.g. standard spatial default damping 0.9 / stiffness 700) versus expressive's bouncier springs; one port lists expressive default spatial damping as 0.73 rather than 0.8, so ports disagree and the AOSP source should be preferred — [motor (pub.dev)](https://pub.dev/documentation/motor/latest/); [material_expressive (pub.dev)](https://pub.dev/documentation/material_expressive/0.8.0/)
- Effects springs are always critically damped (1.0, no overshoot) while spatial springs are underdamped (0.6-0.8): i.e. things that move may bounce, things that fade/recolour never do — [AOSP ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/support/+/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt)
- Pixel Launcher (Android 16 QPR3, Dec 2025) added a springy "jiggle" when a folder closes, replacing a plain shrink — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/12/17/android-16-qpr3-new-folder-animations/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-16-qpr3-bouncy-folders-3646260/)
- One UI 8: a Home Up comparison video showed app-open expanding from the icon "with a soft zoom and fade" and more depth than One UI 7; a leaker said the app-start animation was made "more natural, smoother, and non-linear" — [SamMobile-adjacent report, Sammy Fans](https://www.sammyfans.com/2025/07/21/one-ui-8-home-up-makes-samsung-animations-better-than-one-ui-7-watch/); [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/articles/one-ui-8-bring-fresh-180425682.html)
- Contradicting that, Galaxy S25+ and A-series owners on Samsung's forums said One UI 8 animations feel less smooth / "too rough" / "too fast" than One UI 7; a reply pointed to Good Lock Home Up's "App open animation tuning" — [Samsung Community](https://r2.community.samsung.com/t5/Galaxy-S/Problem-on-one-ui8/m-p/21027200); [Samsung Community (Galaxy A)](https://r1.community.samsung.com/t5/galaxy-a/one-ui-8/td-p/35358586)
- One UI 8.5 beta: search-to-Quick-Panel results now "appear gently instead of popping in" (staggered reveal rather than instant pop) — [Sammy Fans](https://www.sammyfans.com/2026/01/01/one-ui-8-5-gives-search-animations-a-smooth-upgrade-from-one-ui-8-0/)
- iOS 26 Liquid Glass: core trait is seamless shape morphing between controls during transitions, plus specular highlights that react to interaction — [Medium developer handbook](https://medium.com/@jaikrishnavj/mastering-ios-26s-liquid-glass-a-comprehensive-developer-s-handbook-2bba9965b024); [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/what-exactly-is-iphone-liquid-glass_id174090)
- Apple toned down Liquid Glass transparency/distortion after beta feedback, reworked menu-open motion in iOS 26.2 to better match WWDC demos, and in iOS 26.4 added "Reduce Bright Effects" and updated Reduce Motion for Liquid Glass because some users found it "overwhelming, distracting" — [Letem svetem Applem](https://www.letemsvetemapplem.eu/en/2025/11/13/liquid-glass-se-v-ios-26-2-docka-dalsiho-vylepseni-mrknete-jak-skvele-vypada/); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/iphones/ios-26-4-finally-adds-the-hidden-controls-to-tone-down-liquid-glass-heres-how)
- Apple's foundational definition (WWDC18 "Designing Fluid Interfaces"): fluid = instant response, one-to-one tracking, fully redirectable mid-gesture, momentum projection, rubber-banding at boundaries, spatial consistency, hinting; "It's not just about framerate. It's what's in the frames." — [Apple WWDC18 session 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/) (historical, 2018, but still the canonical reference)

### Inferences
- The M3 Expressive spring numbers use the same units as `androidx.dynamicanimation`'s `SpringForce` (stiffness in the Hooke's-law sense, dimensionless damping ratio), so ARES can paste them straight into `SpringForce(...).setStiffness(380f).setDampingRatio(0.8f)` without Compose. (API knowledge: Compose's `Spring.StiffnessMedium` = 1500f equals `SpringForce.STIFFNESS_MEDIUM` = 1500f, which supports this; unverified here.)
- A good ARES token set: "spatial" springs (0.8/380 default, 0.6/800 fast for snappy pops, 0.8/200 slow for large surfaces like the drawer) for anything that moves or scales; "effects" springs (1.0/1600) for alpha, colour, glow intensity, blur radius.
- The One UI 8 complaints suggest that on the owner's own phone, ARES's motion will be judged against Samsung's system motion; matching or slightly softening One UI's pace (not faster) is the safer target.
- Nothing OS 3/4 (dot-matrix, monochrome, glyph-like motion) is aesthetically the closest analogue to ARES's dot-matrix reactor/schematic look, but I found no source describing its motion system (see Gaps).

- No source found on Nothing OS 3/4 motion design or reviewer commentary on its fluidity.
- No measured (frame-timing) review of One UI 8 smoothness; evidence is anecdotal and split.
- No primary Apple HIG text on Liquid Glass motion was retrieved.

## 2. Motion principles: springs vs easing, interruptibility, gesture-driven transitions, shared elements, predictive back, choreography, state motion, haptics

### Takeaway
Fluidity comes from continuity of value and velocity: every animation must start from where the thing is now, at the speed it is moving now, and be retargetable at any frame. Springs give this for free; fixed-duration interpolators do not. Gestures should track 1:1, project momentum on release to choose the destination, rubber-band at limits, and never wait on a timer to detect intent.

### Cited Findings
- Apple: "If you introduce any amount of lag, things all of a sudden just kind of fall off a cliff"; latency matters for taps and presses, not only swipes — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Interfaces should be "fully redirectable"; intent detection should use finger acceleration rather than waiting for velocity to fall below a threshold ("the faster you stop, the faster we can detect it") — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Momentum projection: compute where the object would come to rest using its release velocity and a scroll-like deceleration rate, then snap to the nearest valid target; applies to position, scale and rotation — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Springs reduced to two designer controls, damping ratio and response; avoid "duration" for elastic behaviours; start at 100% damping and add bounce only when the gesture carries momentum ("if a gesture has momentum, and there isn't any overshoot, it can often feel broken"); Apple Music: tap to open Now Playing = 100% damping, swipe to dismiss = 80% — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Rubber-banding: keep tracking past boundaries with resistance; without it users can't tell a frozen phone from one at the edge — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Spatial consistency: content should leave and return along the same path; hinting: transitions should grow toward the final state in the direction of the finger — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Swipe hysteresis "is usually 10 points in iOS" — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Motion blur/stretching adds information per frame; frame content matters, not just frame rate — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Android predictive back: on Android 15 the developer option was removed and back-to-home, cross-task and cross-activity animations are on by default for apps that opt in (`android:enableOnBackInvokedCallback`, app or activity level); on Android 16+, `OnBackInvokedCallback` with `PRIORITY_SYSTEM_NAVIGATION_OBSERVER` observes back without consuming it, so the system back-to-home animation still plays — [Android Developers: predictive back](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/custom-back/predictive-back-gesture)
- Consuming back with `PRIORITY_DEFAULT`/`PRIORITY_OVERLAY` disables the system predictive animation, so the app must animate itself — [Android Developers: predictive back](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/custom-back/predictive-back-gesture)
- M3 Expressive separates spatial springs (may overshoot) from effects springs (never overshoot) — [AOSP ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/support/+/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt)
- One UI 8.5's polish is a stagger: results "appear gently" rather than popping in together — [Sammy Fans](https://www.sammyfans.com/2026/01/01/one-ui-8-5-gives-search-animations-a-smooth-upgrade-from-one-ui-8-0/)

### Inferences
- For ARES's HomePager, drawer swipe and bottom sheets: track the finger 1:1 (no animation during drag), take `VelocityTracker` velocity on ACTION_UP, project the endpoint (Apple-style projection, or simply `FlingAnimation` friction maths), choose page/open/closed from the projected point rather than a distance threshold, then hand the current value + velocity to a `SpringAnimation`. This is the single biggest "stiff to fluid" change.
- Use damping 1.0 for tap-triggered opens (power sheet, colour sheet) and ~0.75-0.8 for fling-triggered settles (pager snaps, drawer release, drag-drop landings), mirroring Apple's tap-vs-swipe rule.
- Overscroll at the ends of the pager/drawer should rubber-band (diminishing-return offset such as `limit * (1 - 1/(x*c/limit + 1))`, the commonly-cited iOS formula; formula from general knowledge, unverified here) and spring back, rather than stopping dead.
- Assistant state motion (idle / listening / thinking / speaking) on the reactor should cross-fade parameters with effects springs (critically damped) so state changes never "pop": animate target values (ring speed, glow, hue) with springs, and let the per-frame draw read the sprung values.
- Haptic pairing: tie haptic ticks to physical events in the motion (detent crossed, page commit, drop landed, spring settled) rather than to the start of an animation; ARES's `HoldDetector` already ticks each eighth and fires a heavy tick at commit, which matches this principle.
- Stagger: when the drawer or board appears, stagger rows by ~15-30 ms each using the same spring; stagger reads as "fluid" whereas everything appearing at once reads as "barebones". (Number is a design rule of thumb, not sourced.)

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched for Google's own haptics guidance (`HapticFeedbackConstants`, Android haptics design principles) or for M3 Expressive's stagger/choreography guidance.
- The WWDC18 transcript did not include its exact rubber-band or projection code; the projection formula in the Apple summary is a reconstruction.
- No 2025-2026 WWDC talk on fluid motion was retrieved (e.g. Liquid Glass sessions), so Apple's latest stated principles are only via secondary coverage.

## 3. Android implementation with Views

### Takeaway
Everything needed for fluid motion exists in the Views stack: `androidx.dynamicanimation` (SpringAnimation/FlingAnimation, retargetable, velocity-carrying), `TransitionManager.controlDelayedTransition` for gesture-scrubbed layout transitions, the predictive-back progress callbacks, `RenderEffect` blur/shaders and AGSL `RuntimeShader` for glass/glow. What a third-party launcher cannot do is drive the system's app-open/close window animation the way Pixel's QuickStep does; that path is privileged.

### Cited Findings
- `androidx.dynamicanimation:dynamicanimation` (doc shows 1.0.0): `SpringAnimation(view, DynamicAnimation.TRANSLATION_Y, final)`, properties ALPHA, TRANSLATION_X/Y/Z, ROTATION(_X/_Y), SCROLL_X/Y, SCALE_X/Y, X/Y/Z; `SpringForce` with `DAMPING_RATIO_HIGH_BOUNCY/MEDIUM_BOUNCY(default)/LOW_BOUNCY/NO_BOUNCY` and `STIFFNESS_HIGH/MEDIUM(default)/LOW/VERY_LOW` — [Android Developers: spring animation](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/spring-animation)
- `setStartVelocity(px/s)` (take it from `VelocityTracker.computeCurrentVelocity(1000)`); `animateToFinalPosition()` retargets a running spring at any time (interruptible) and starts it if idle; values update on each animation pulse before draw; must be called on the main thread — [Android Developers: spring animation](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/spring-animation)
- Registering an `OnAnimationUpdateListener` "can keep the animation from running on a separate thread"; add it only when needed. `skipToEnd()` causes a visual jump; `cancel()` stops in place; `setMinValue/MaxValue` clamp — [Android Developers: spring animation](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/spring-animation)
- The same page states Compose is the recommended toolkit and links to Compose's spring `AnimationSpec` — [Android Developers: spring animation](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/spring-animation)
- Predictive back in Views: AndroidX Activity 1.8.0+ `OnBackPressedCallback.handleOnBackStarted/handleOnBackProgressed/handleOnBackCancelled` (backward compatible); platform `OnBackAnimationCallback` (`onBackStarted/onBackProgressed(BackEvent)/onBackCancelled`) is API 34+, registered via `OnBackInvokedDispatcher` (`View#findOnBackInvokedDispatcher()`) — [Android Developers: predictive back in Views](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/custom-back/support-animations-views); [AOSP OnBackAnimationCallback.java mirror](https://git.aicp-rom.com/frameworks_base.git/plain/core/java/android/window/OnBackAnimationCallback.java)
- Gesture-scrubbed View transitions: AndroidX Transitions 1.5.0+ `TransitionManager.controlDelayedTransition` (instead of `beginDelayedTransition`), create in `handleOnBackStarted`, map `currentFraction` to `BackEvent.progress` in `handleOnBackProgressed`, `animateToEnd()` on commit, reset on cancel — [Android Developers: predictive back in Views](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/custom-back/support-animations-views)
- `OnBackPressedCallback` needs AndroidX Activity 1.6.0+; Compose `PredictiveBackHandler` needs activity-compose 1.8.0+ — [Android Developers: predictive back](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/custom-back/predictive-back-gesture)
- AGSL: `RuntimeShader(src)` compiles on construction; use as a `Paint.shader` in `onDraw` (fill, circles, even text); update uniforms per frame with `setFloatUniform`/`setColorUniform` (string-named; mismatches fail silently); `view.setRenderEffect(RenderEffect.createRuntimeShaderEffect(shader, "background"))` applies a shader to a View and all its children, with `background.eval(fragCoord)` sampling the rendered content — [Android Developers: using AGSL](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/graphics/agsl/using-agsl)
- `RenderEffect` is "more expensive than drawing a custom View" and only affects its own hierarchy — [Android Developers: using AGSL](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/graphics/agsl/using-agsl)
- `ActivityOptions.makeScaleUpAnimation(source, startX, startY, w, h)` scales the new activity up from a region of a source View; ActivityOptions dates from API 16 — [Android ActivityOptionsCompat reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/core/app/ActivityOptionsCompat); [ActivityOptions reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/android/app/ActivityOptions?hl=he)
- Launcher3 Quickstep uses `RemoteAnimationProvider`/app-transition-manager plumbing and an `ActivityOptionsWrapper` for widget launches — [/e/ BlissLauncher3 (Launcher3 fork) commit](https://gitlab.e.foundation/e/os/BlissLauncher3/-/commit/42431d521702d5eddeb14de593bbd24e2496eaa9)
- Lawnchair (the leading open-source Launcher3 derivative) states "QuickSwitch support for Android Recents integration on Android 15-16 (root required)", and that Play builds may restrict features — [Lawnchair GitHub](https://github.com/LawnchairLauncher/lawnchair)

### Inferences
- (API knowledge, unverified here) Min SDKs: `RenderEffect.createBlurEffect` API 31; `RuntimeShader`/AGSL and `RenderEffect.createRuntimeShaderEffect` API 33; `WindowInsetsAnimation.Callback` API 30 (AndroidX `WindowInsetsAnimationCompat` backports it); `ActivityOptions.makeClipRevealAnimation` API 23; `makeScaleUpAnimation` API 16; `View.setRequestedFrameRate` API 35. All are within ARES's range since the target phone runs Android 16.
- (API knowledge, unverified here) `SpringForce` numeric constants: STIFFNESS_HIGH 10000, MEDIUM 1500, LOW 200, VERY_LOW 50; DAMPING_RATIO_HIGH_BOUNCY 0.2, MEDIUM_BOUNCY 0.5, LOW_BOUNCY 0.75, NO_BOUNCY 1.0. The default (0.5 / 1500) is much bouncier than any M3 token; ARES should always set explicit values.
- Launcher app open: a third-party launcher cannot run the QuickStep-style "icon grows into the live app window and back on close" animation (that uses privileged remote-animation/shell-transition APIs; Lawnchair needs root even for recents). ARES can: (a) pass `ActivityOptions.makeClipRevealAnimation`/`makeScaleUpAnimation` from the tapped icon's bounds when calling `startActivity`, so the system animates from the icon; (b) animate its own icon (press-scale spring to ~0.92 on DOWN, release on UP) and dim/scale the home content as it hands off; (c) on return (onResume / onNewIntent for HOME), play an in-launcher "settle" (content springs from 0.94 scale/alpha back to 1). One UI's own app-close animation targets whichever launcher is default only if the launcher cooperates through private APIs, so expect a generic close animation.
- Glass/glow for ARES: blur behind sheets with `RenderEffect.createBlurEffect` on a snapshot/backdrop View (not on the live reactor every frame), or a cheap fake glass (pre-blurred wallpaper bitmap + tint + AGSL edge highlight). An AGSL shader is a good fit for the reactor glow and an "edge glow" listening state since uniforms (level, hue, time) can be driven by springs.
- Keyboard: drawer search should use `WindowInsetsAnimationCompat.Callback` so the search field rides up with the IME frame by frame instead of jumping.
- Drag reordering on the board: lift with a spring (scale 1.05, elevation/shadow up), make neighbours slide with `animateToFinalPosition` springs on each hover change (retargeting, never restarting), and drop with a spatial spring into the slot. CLAUDE.md notes that rebuilding the row CANCELs the touched view; animating translation on existing views instead of rebuilding would avoid that and look smoother.
- `MotionLayout` (ConstraintLayout 2.x) can scrub keyframed layouts from a gesture, but it is duration/keyframe based and largely in maintenance as Google invests in Compose; for ARES's few hand-built surfaces, springs + translation are simpler. (Status of MotionLayout not verified from a 2026 source.)

## 4. Performance for fluidity

### Takeaway
At 120 Hz the budget is ~8.3 ms per frame; Android 15+ adaptive refresh rate (ARR) votes per View and drops idle content to lower rates, so a launcher should explicitly request HIGH only while gestures/springs run and let the ambient reactor render at 30-60 fps. Measure with JankStats in the app and Perfetto/Macrobenchmark on the real Galaxy, not the emulator.

### Cited Findings
- ARR (Android 15 QPR1+ on devices with HAL support, check `Display.hasArrSupport()`): apps render at a lower rate by default and boost when needed; the display matches content rate — [Android Developers: adaptive refresh rate](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/adaptive-refresh-rate)
- Per-View votes: `view.requestedFrameRate = REQUESTED_FRAME_RATE_CATEGORY_HIGH / NORMAL (~60 Hz) / NO_PREFERENCE / DEFAULT` or an explicit rate (30f, 60f, 120f); only Views being redrawn vote; highest wins; setting it on a ViewGroup does not propagate to children — [Android Developers: ARR](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/adaptive-refresh-rate)
- Default policy: ACTION_DOWN boosts to High for a while after release; fling rate drops as velocity slows; app launch and window transitions get a brief boost; Views changing position/size get a higher rate automatically; SurfaceView/TextureView explicit rates are respected — [Android Developers: ARR](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/adaptive-refresh-rate)
- Custom scrollers should call `view.frameContentVelocity = px/s` every frame of a fling (it resets on redraw); built in for RecyclerView, ScrollView, etc. (RecyclerView 1.4.0, core 1.15.0) — [Android Developers: ARR](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/adaptive-refresh-rate)
- Disabling touch boost (`window.isFrameRateBoostOnTouchEnabled = false`) or ARR (`isFrameRatePowerSavingsBalanced = false`) is not recommended; slow/static animations can run below Normal to save power; avoid forcing High needlessly — [Android Developers: ARR](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/adaptive-refresh-rate)
- JankStats: `androidx.metrics:metrics-performance` 1.0.0; FrameMetrics-based on API 24+; a frame is jank when it takes 2x the refresh period (tunable `jankHeuristicMultiplier`); `PerformanceMetricsState.putState("HomePager","Swiping")` tags frames; `FrameDataApi31.frameOverrunNanos` on API 31+; toggle tracking in onResume/onPause — [Android Developers: JankStats](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/jankstats)
- Baseline Profiles improve code execution speed by about 30% from first launch by avoiding interpretation/JIT for included paths — [Android Developers: Compose performance](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/performance)
- Per WWDC18, perceived fluidity depends on latency and on frame content (motion blur/stretch), not only fps — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)

### Inferences
- Frame budgets (arithmetic): 120 Hz = 8.33 ms, 90 Hz = 11.1 ms, 60 Hz = 16.7 ms; JankStats' default 2x rule flags >16.7 ms frames at 120 Hz, so also log `frameOverrunNanos` to catch single missed vsyncs that are still visible at 120 Hz.
- ARES's reactor `maxFps` (30 on home / 20 elsewhere) should be expressed as `requestedFrameRate = 30f` (or NORMAL) on the reactor View so ARR can actually lower the panel rate; when the user touches, pages, or drags, the touch boost lifts everything to High automatically. Set the reactor to HIGH only during transient effects (hold-to-talk charge, state change bursts).
- A self-throttled invalidate() loop at 30 fps on a 120 Hz panel can look juddery during page swipes (the reactor moves at 120 Hz with the pager but animates internally at 30). During pager drags, either let the reactor animate at the display rate or freeze its internal animation; avoid mixed cadence.
- onDraw hygiene (API knowledge): no allocations (pre-create Paint/Path/RectF/shaders), no `saveLayer` unless needed (offscreen buffers are expensive), cache static parts (the dot-matrix schematic) in a `RenderNode` (API 29+) or bitmap and redraw only animated layers, use `setLayerType(LAYER_TYPE_HARDWARE)` on views during property animations of alpha/scale (ViewPropertyAnimator's `withLayer()`), and keep overdraw low (no window background under full-bleed reactor; check with "Debug GPU overdraw").
- RenderThread: `ViewPropertyAnimator` and RenderNode property changes (translation/scale/alpha/rotation) are applied without re-running onDraw, so prefer animating View properties over redrawing Canvas content for moves/fades. Custom per-frame Canvas drawing (the reactor) does run on the UI thread each frame; if it gets heavy, move it to a `SurfaceView` with its own render loop (a separate surface composited by SurfaceFlinger, unaffected by UI-thread hiccups) or an AGSL shader where the GPU does the per-pixel work. TextureView costs an extra GPU copy and is usually worse than SurfaceView for this.
- Measurement plan: JankStats in debug builds with states per surface; a Macrobenchmark module (FrameTimingMetric) scripting "swipe to page 2, open drawer, scroll, fling back" on the real Galaxy; Perfetto/`adb shell dumpsys gfxinfo com.dhruv.ares framestats` for spot checks; Android GPU Inspector for shader cost. The emulator's software GPU numbers (27 fps) are not representative.
- Samsung specifics: Galaxy S-series panels are LTPO adaptive (1-120 Hz). One UI's "Motion smoothness: Adaptive" setting plus Samsung's own policies sit on top of ARR; a launcher cannot force 120 Hz when the user chose "Standard" (60 Hz). Game Booster/Game Optimizing Service throttling applies to apps classified as games, not to launchers. (From general knowledge, not verified in this session.)

- No public 2026 data on launcher-specific jank benchmarks (Pixel Launcher vs third-party).

## 5. Should ARES migrate to Jetpack Compose or stay on Views?

### Takeaway
Google now says Compose (1.9+) matches Views on scroll jank and is the recommended toolkit, and Compose has first-class spring/MotionScheme/shared-element/predictive-back APIs. But none of the fluidity techniques ARES needs are Compose-only, the launcher's hottest path is custom Canvas/AGSL drawing that is identical in both, and Views remain pre-compiled in the system image. A targeted Views polish is the faster route; Compose is reasonable only for new self-contained surfaces (e.g. sheets) via `ComposeView`.

### Cited Findings
- "Compose 1.9 and later match the Views performance for jank while scrolling" (Google hero benchmarks on the Pokedex Views vs Compose apps); page updated 2026-09-11 — [Android Developers: Compose performance](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/performance)
- The same page gives no cold-start comparison; it recommends an app-specific Baseline Profile on top of the one Compose ships — [Android Developers: Compose performance](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/performance)
- Views are part of the system image and pre-compiled, so they never go through JIT; this is why a cold Compose app can start slower without a profile (community discussion from earlier Compose releases, anecdotal) — [Kotlin Slack archive](https://slack-chats.kotlinlang.org/t/2459428/is-having-janky-frames-normal-with-jetpack-compose-i-build-t)
- Adding Compose to the Sunflower sample grew the APK from 2,252 KB to 3,034 KB (+782 KB) — [Android Developers: compare Compose and View metrics](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/migrate/compare-metrics)
- The Views spring-animation docs themselves now point to Compose as the recommended toolkit — [Android Developers: spring animation](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/spring-animation)
- Compose has `PredictiveBackHandler` (activity-compose 1.8.0+) and `Modifier.preferredFrameRate` (Compose 1.9) — [Android Developers: predictive back](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/custom-back/predictive-back-gesture); [Android Developers: ARR](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/adaptive-refresh-rate)
- M3 Expressive's motion tokens are implemented in Compose Material 3 (`ExpressiveMotionTokens`) — [AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/support/+/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/ExpressiveMotionTokens.kt)

### Inferences
- For Views: the owner's complaint ("stiff, barebones") is about motion design (no velocity hand-off, no springs, no stagger, no press feedback), not toolkit limits. All fixes in section 3 are available in Views today, and ARES's HomePager, Reorder, HomeGrid and DragFrame are custom touch code that would need a full rewrite in Compose.
- For Compose: Compose makes shared-element/container transforms (`SharedTransitionLayout`), `AnimatedContent`, M3 Expressive components (shape-morphing buttons, loading indicators) and `MotionScheme` trivial; if ARES later wants many card-to-detail transitions, Compose saves real effort. Interop is incremental: a `ComposeView` can host one sheet or card inside the existing Views tree.
- Recommended: stay on Views for the launcher shell, pager, drawer, board and reactor; adopt a small internal "motion kit" (spring token constants copied from M3 Expressive, a gesture-to-spring helper, a stagger helper). Revisit Compose only for new, isolated surfaces.
- Launcher examples: Launcher3/Pixel Launcher and Lawnchair are Views-based (Lawnchair adds Compose for settings screens, per general knowledge; not verified here), which indicates Views remain the practical choice for launcher home surfaces.

### Gaps
- No found 2026 cold-start benchmark Compose vs Views, and no benchmark specific to launchers or heavy custom Canvas drawing.
- Not verified which parts of Lawnchair or Pixel Launcher use Compose in 2026.

## 6. Fluid assistant presence (Gemini overlay, Siri edge glow, Galaxy AI) and voice-to-card-to-app transitions

### Takeaway
System assistants have moved from a full-screen takeover or a fixed orb to a light that frames the current context (Siri's border glow reacting to voice, Gemini's glowing floating panel), and keep the user's app visible underneath. Google continues iterating toward waveform animations and overlays that no longer open a new screen. ARES's reactor plus captions fits this direction if it appears in place (glow/edge light, voice-reactive) and results grow out of it rather than replacing the screen.

### Cited Findings
- Siri (iOS 18.1+, Apple Intelligence devices: iPhone 15 Pro and 16 family) replaced the orb with an edge-lit glow around the whole screen that reacts to the user's voice; older devices kept the orb; reviewers note it is a UI change, not a capability change — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/11/03/new-apple-intelligence-siri-looks-different-works-the-same/); [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1865686/iphone-glowing-around-edges-reason/)
- Gemini on Android uses a floating overlay panel with a glow around the panel to signal context awareness, deliberately not full screen so it works alongside the current app; early versions resembled Siri's glow with a more indigo palette — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/08/23/gemini-glow-floating-overlay/); [Notebookcheck (Android Authority teardown)](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Gemini-AI-assistant-takes-animation-design-cues-from-Apple-s-new-Siri-in-the-latest-Google-app-beta-APK-teardown.872459.0.html)
- April 2026: Gemini overlay moved to thinner icons, a waveform animation and a new sheet; Gemini Live no longer opens a new screen when activated. July 2026: the Live overlay animation was made "sharper and cleaner", and its sparkle-and-dots effect appeared in Circle to Search — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-overlay-and-live-ui-changes-3655577/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-live-circle-search-new-animations-3692272/)
- Android 16 QPR1 beta tested a system Gemini launch animation in which a black overlay expands inward from the screen borders before the overlay appears (limited A/B test on Pixels) — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/06/14/android-16-qpr1-beta-gemini-launch/); [HuaweiCentral](https://www.huaweicentral.com/android-16-qpr1-beta-2-shows-a-refreshed-gemini-launch-animation/amp/)
- Reports (unconfirmed by Apple) say iOS 27 replaces the edge glow with a glowing pill-shaped orb expanding from the Dynamic Island — [FeedBagel](https://feedbagel.com/post/siri-uses-pill-shape-to-hide-the-dynamic-island-in-ios-27-but-iphone-18-could-ma)
- Apple's hinting principle: things should grow from their origin toward their final state — [WWDC18 803](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)

### Inferences
- ARES's floating bubble and home-screen hold-to-talk can borrow the "presence as light" pattern: on summon, an AGSL edge glow (or a ring around the bubble) whose intensity follows the microphone level through an effects spring; thinking = slow rotation/shimmer; speaking = waveform tied to TTS amplitude. Keep the user's current screen visible (overlay, not activity switch) like Gemini Live's 2026 behaviour.
- Voice to card to app: grow result cards out of the reactor/bubble's position (container-transform style: animate bounds + corner radius from the orb's circle to the card rect with a spatial spring), and when a result opens an app, launch with `makeClipRevealAnimation` from the card's bounds so the motion stays continuous across the hand-off.
- Captions should fade/slide in with effects springs and stagger by word or phrase, matching the Mac side's caption scheduling.

### Gaps
- No source found describing Galaxy AI / Bixby's 2025-2026 summon animation on One UI 8.
- No primary design write-up from Google or Apple explaining the rationale for the glow-based presence; findings are from news coverage and teardowns.
- Gemini overlay animation specifics (durations, spring parameters) are not public.
